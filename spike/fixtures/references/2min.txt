I wanted to write down a few thoughts about the trip while they are still fresh. We left early on Saturday, a little after six, and the roads were almost empty until we reached the coast. The first stop was a small bakery that a friend had recommended, and it was every bit as good as promised. We bought far too much bread and ate most of it in the car.

The house itself was smaller than it looked in the pictures, but the view made up for it. From the kitchen window you could see the whole bay, and in the evening the light turned the water a deep orange. On the second day it rained from morning until night, so we stayed inside, played cards, and argued about the rules of a game that nobody could quite remember.

On Monday the weather cleared and we walked the long path along the cliffs. It took nearly four hours, mostly because we kept stopping to look at birds and to take photographs that will never be as good as the real thing. Near the end of the walk we found a narrow beach with nobody else on it, and we sat there for a long time without saying much.

What I noticed most was how quickly the days settled into a rhythm. Coffee, a walk, something simple for lunch, a book in the afternoon, and a slow dinner. Nobody checked their messages very often, and nothing bad happened as a result. I would like to remember that the next time a week at work feels impossible.

We drove home on Wednesday in heavy traffic, tired and a little sunburned, already talking about when we could go back. If we do, I would stay longer, bring better shoes, and book the bakery order in advance.
